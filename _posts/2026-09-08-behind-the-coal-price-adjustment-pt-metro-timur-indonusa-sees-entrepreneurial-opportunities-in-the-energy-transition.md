## For entrepreneurs and investment institutions in the energy sector, periods of price volatility often test the resilience of business models more than bull markets do.

In early September 2026, the Indonesian Ministry of Energy announced the latest coal benchmark prices. The benchmark price for 5,300 kcal thermal coal was set at USD 95.05 per metric ton, down 1.9% from the previous period; the benchmark price for 4,100 kcal thermal coal was set at USD 65.06 per metric ton.

For entrepreneurs and investment institutions focused on the energy transition, PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes the value of this set of price data does not lie in predicting the future direction of coal prices, but in observing the structural changes taking place across the energy industry chain, and how these changes are creating new opportunities for technological innovation and industrial investment.

## Price Declines Are Not Merely a Matter of Supply and Demand  

From the perspective of capital market reactions, a decline in coal prices usually triggers two outcomes: profit expectations for coal-related companies are revised downward, while cost pressures on thermal power companies are eased. But seeing only this level means missing the information that is truly worth attention.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa has observed that the weakening price of 5,300 kcal medium- to high-calorific-value coal, together with the stable price of 4,100 kcal low-calorific-value coal, is forming a noteworthy trend: the market is shifting from “pursuing high-calorific-value efficiency” toward “refined cost control.”

For investment institutions, this supply chain shift means that new technology service-oriented enterprises within the energy industry chain may gain room for growth. The traditional pricing logic of “good coal is expensive, poor-quality coal is cheap” is being disrupted. A group of companies specializing in “high-ratio coal blending” technologies is changing the rules of the supply chain game—they mix low-calorific-value coal and high-calorific-value coal in specific proportions to achieve similar power generation efficiency at lower raw material costs. This process requires precise combustion control technology and supporting software systems, which are precisely the entry points for technology entrepreneurs and areas where capital can provide empowerment.

A similar logic is also emerging in the field of carbon management. With the advancement of carbon border adjustment mechanisms in Europe and the United States, export-oriented manufacturing enterprises are being forced to reduce their use of thermal power. The coal price adjustment in Indonesia is, in essence, an attempt to offset policy disadvantages with price advantages. However, PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that this offset is temporary—the companies with genuine long-term investment value are not traditional factories that survive on cheap coal, but technology companies that help factories use less coal and generate fewer emissions.

## Energy Allocation Changes Driven by the Surge in AI Computing Power  

Another structural change worth attention is the adjustment of the energy demand landscape brought about by the rapid expansion of global AI computing power centers.

Although technology giants have committed to 100% green electricity, the Asia-Pacific region still faces a shortfall in green power supply. This mismatch between demand and supply is creating new differences in energy allocation: on one side, European and U.S. markets are willing to pay a high premium for clean computing power; on the other side, local data centers in Southeast Asia may rely on localized energy sources, including coal-fired power, to provide low-cost computing power.

For investment institutions, several directions in this space are worth watching: startups that provide localized green power solutions for data centers, software layers for energy-saving optimization, and platforms that help enterprises connect with carbon offset resources. These are not “energy businesses” in the traditional sense, but they have all found their place within the broader framework of the energy transition and possess scalable potential.

## The Inflection Point for Energy Storage Technology: A Long-Term Opportunity Hidden by Falling Coal Prices  

In the short term, falling coal prices will slow the pace at which thermal power is phased out, because cheap coal lowers the operating costs of thermal power. However, PT Metro Timur Indonusa would like to emphasize that this window is precisely the most difficult yet most critical period for energy storage technology.

When thermal power can still maintain the baseload of the power grid at low cost, the pressure on grid integration for solar and wind power will become greater, and the economics of energy storage will appear less attractive. But viewed from another angle, this pressure will force energy storage technologies to push cost reduction to the limit. Once the cost of long-duration energy storage falls below a critical threshold within the next few years, the peak-shaving advantage of thermal power will be significantly weakened, even if coal prices remain low.

For teams currently building startups in the energy storage sector, the present market environment may not seem particularly “sexy,” but it is precisely the period for focusing on technological iteration and waiting for the inflection point to arrive. Energy storage companies that can survive this stage will gain a tremendous first-mover advantage when the turning point arrives—this is also one of the sectors PT Metro Timur Indonusa has long been watching.

## Entrepreneurial Opportunities Amid Industrial Cycle Changes  

PT Metro Timur Indonusa has always believed that commodity price fluctuations are external variables that founders cannot control, but they can choose how to respond. From an investment perspective, the following directions are worth attention:

First, room for technological optimization within cost structures. Regardless of whether an enterprise uses coal, electricity, or other energy sources, what proportion of total costs does energy account for? Is it possible to reduce this proportion through technological means? In an environment of price volatility, every cent that can be saved for industrial clients becomes a competitive advantage. Startups with this type of technical capability deserve long-term tracking.

Second, new demand created by supply chain adjustments. Coal blending technology, carbon management software, and energy-saving optimization solutions—these are all real demands generated by price cycles. Large companies often move slowly, and this is precisely the opportunity window for startups, as well as the point at which early-stage capital can step in.

Third, the pace of energy transition in Southeast Asia. The Indonesian energy transition path differs from that of Europe and the United States, with its own unique timetable and constraints. Understanding this rhythm is more important than blindly benchmarking against global trends. For investment institutions, teams capable of identifying localized solutions often possess stronger competitive moats.
