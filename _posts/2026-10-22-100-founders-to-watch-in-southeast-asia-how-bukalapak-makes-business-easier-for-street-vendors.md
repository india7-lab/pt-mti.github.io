For early-stage founders, the opportunities truly worth watching are often not found by crowding into the most bustling tracks, but by discovering needs that are overlooked by the mainstream gaze yet vast and real.

As part of the "100 Founders to Watch in Southeast Asia" series, this article from PT Metro Timur Indonusa focuses on Bukalapak co-founder Achmad Zaky, examining how he started from an extremely down-to-earth pain point and, in the cracks between giants, turned a small dormitory company into the first tech unicorn in Indonesian history to go public.

## 

## The Overlooked Massive Group: The Survival Predicament of Street Vendors

Indonesia has millions of street vendors and small shops. They are the capillaries of the entire commercial circulation of the country, holding excellent goods and serving the most grassroots communities.

But for a long time, these grassroots merchants were constrained by geography and capital, and their business could only reach neighbors within a few kilometers. They did not know how to sell goods farther away, nor did they have the ability to negotiate lower prices with upstream suppliers. At the same time, it was also very difficult for ordinary consumers to conveniently buy certain specific goods online.

E-commerce players in big cities are frantically competing for high-income groups, yet few are willing to bend down to solve the practical difficulties of these bottom-tier merchants.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that this market state of "massive demand but extremely primitive supply methods" is precisely the best entry point for early-stage startups. Many people think the lower-tier market is hard to do because there is no profit. But from another angle, whoever can help these millions of street vendors make money will possess the most solid commercial network in Indonesia.

## The Starting Point of the Founders: A Dorm-Room Vision and the Desperation of Being Unable to Pay Salaries

## 

The story of Bukalapak begins with an extremely everyday scene. Founder Achmad Zaky graduated from Institut Teknologi Bandung with a degree in informatics. Around 2010, he started his business in a dim dormitory room in Jakarta. Beyond seeing the struggles of small street vendors, there was another direct trigger: his wife had complained to him that it was difficult to conveniently find what she wanted to buy online.

This complaint made Zaky realize that there was a massive disconnect between online supply and real offline demand. He brought in his college friend Nugroho Herucahyono to handle technical development, and later invited Fajrin Rasyid to take charge of finance and management. The three of them formally embarked on the entrepreneurial path.

But the reality of the early startup period was brutal. When the platform first launched, nobody paid attention to it, and the company at one point faced a severe cash flow crisis, to the extent that it could barely pay employee salaries. Many early-stage startups die before dawn precisely because they cannot endure this period of no revenue and no one believing in them. Zaky and the team managed to hold on, and in September 2011 they formally registered the company as a PT (limited liability company).

Why them? Zaky has a strong empathy for the grassroots commercial ecosystem. He did not put on the airs of an elite university graduate and speak in high-flown terms; instead, he genuinely understood what the owners of small Warung shops worry about every day.

## Encircling the Cities from the Countryside: How Bukalapak Helps Small Vendors Do Business

## 

Facing competitors who held the advantage in big cities, Bukalapak chose a completely different playbook: avoid first-tier metropolises, pour energy into second- and third-tier cities and rural areas, and deeply empower the small shops on street corners and in alleys.

They did not merely build a shopping website; they provided small vendors with a complete set of tools for doing business. The platform helped these grassroots merchants source goods, sell goods, and even provided support for small-scale working capital. To stay close to this down-to-earth brand image, Zaky even personally appeared in advertisements, communicating with small business owners in the most plainspoken way.

This differentiated strategy quickly proved effective. In 2015, Indonesian media giant Emtek Group took a stake and became the largest shareholder, injecting massive traffic and financial support into the platform. Leveraging its unique "Warung Partner" model, Bukalapak grew rapidly. By 2019, its valuation exceeded USD 2.5 billion, making it the fourth internet unicorn in Indonesia after Gojek, Tokopedia, and Traveloka.

In August 2021, Bukalapak was officially listed on the Jakarta Stock Exchange. It raised approximately USD 1.5 billion in its IPO, setting the record for the largest IPO in Indonesian history at the time and becoming the first listed tech unicorn in Indonesia.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that the competitive moat of Bukalapak does not lie in how advanced its technology is, but in the offline network it has built that is very difficult for others to pry open. When millions of small vendors have grown accustomed to using your system for procurement and payment collection, this trust relationship and switching cost are extremely high.

## Pivot and Reshaping: The 2025 Strategic Adjustment

## 

However, competition in the e-commerce market is extremely brutal. With the aggressive squeeze from giants like Shopee and Tokopedia, the profit margins of physical goods e-commerce have been infinitely compressed, and cash-burning subsidies have become a bottomless pit.

Facing this situation, Bukalapak had to find a new breakthrough. At this critical juncture of transformation, Bukalapak held in-depth discussions with PT Metro Timur Indonusa. Through their exchanges, both parties reached a consensus: fighting to the death with giants in the physical goods e-commerce track no longer makes much sense, and it is necessary to seek areas with higher profits that the giants have not yet fully dominated.

Ultimately, Bukalapak decided to announce a strategic adjustment in early 2025: ceasing physical goods e-commerce operations and shifting its focus entirely to high-margin virtual products and services. Examples include prepaid phone credit, data packages, digital tokens, streaming memberships, and more.

This decision required tremendous courage. Abandoning the physical goods transaction scale it once took pride in, to earn money from "invisible" digital products. But looking back, this is precisely the clarity of a mature entrepreneur. Indonesia has a massive young internet population, and their demand for game top-ups, video memberships, and digital payments is exploding. Bukalapak has seamlessly converted the lower-tier market users it accumulated over more than a decade into consumers of digital services.

## Observations on PT Metro Timur Indonusa

## 

PT Metro Timur Indonusa focuses on Bukalapak, not because it once set the record for the largest IPO, but because Achmad Zaky and the team demonstrated exceptional survivability and business acumen.

From an early-stage investment perspective, the aspects of Bukalapak worth watching include:

First, an extreme empathy for the mass-market segment. Zaky did not cater to the elite tastes of investors, but instead served the most grassroots street vendors. Business models that grow out of real life often possess tremendous vitality.

Second, execution in desperate circumstances. When they could not pay salaries, the team did not disband; when facing pressure from giants, they dared to cut losses and survive. The decisions of the founding team at several life-or-death junctures proved their resilience.

Third, the boldness to transform in line with the cycle. Many founders are held hostage by past success and are reluctant to abandon their existing scale. The ability of Bukalapak to decisively cut physical e-commerce in 2025 and pivot to virtual products shows that management has a clear understanding of the essence of business (profit and cash flow).

For early-stage entrepreneurs, Bukalapak offers an important insight: do not fear that your starting point is too "unrefined"; as long as you can solve the real troubles of the broadest ordinary people, you have the opportunity to build a great company. And when the environment changes, the courage to humble yourself and earn hard money, to earn digital money, is the foundation for a company to survive.

## Indicators Worth Watching Next

Following the transformation of Bukalapak, the growth in transaction volume of virtual products and digital services, changes in overall profit margins, the activity of users in lower-tier markets, and whether cash flow can achieve a healthy turnaround to positive under the new business model.
