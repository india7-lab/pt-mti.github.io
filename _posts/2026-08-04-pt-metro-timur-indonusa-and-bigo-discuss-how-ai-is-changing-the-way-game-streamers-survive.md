This week, PT Metro Timur Indonusa was invited by global live-streaming platform Bigo to participate in its closed-door industry seminar. The two parties held in-depth discussions on the game live-streaming ecosystem, streamer development systems, and the impact of AI technology on the content industry.

The Senior Operations Lead at Bigo, Satriya Tian, shared his observations on the Indonesian market during the seminar: game streamers are shifting from “passively signing contracts” to “actively choosing platforms.” Competition among platforms has moved beyond competing for traffic and users, and has extended into the level of “retaining streamers.”

## What Is Happening in the Indonesian Market

Indonesia has more than 150 million gamers and is one of the fastest-growing game live-streaming markets in the world. However, Bigo mentioned one phenomenon: users are mobile, and a viewer can have three live-streaming apps installed at the same time. The real competition takes place around streamers who can continuously produce content.

In Indonesia, the most unavoidable competitor of Bigo is Nimo TV. This Southeast Asian local platform focused on the “gaming” label from the very beginning and signed a group of mid-tier streamers in Indonesia. What it offered was not a one-time signing fee, but a long-term package including traffic support, guild operations, and revenue sharing. As a result, the cost for streamers to switch platforms has increased.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa introduced Twitch as a reference point during the seminar: Twitch was able to make “game live streaming equal Twitch” in the United States not by relying on money, but through its subscription system, diversified commercialization, and creator growth path. Streamers stay because there is stable income and a clear future.

However, PT Metro Timur Indonusa particularly emphasized that Indonesia is not a smaller version of the United States. The credit card payments, high willingness to pay, and gaming culture in the United States are not fully present in Indonesia. The experience of Twitch may be referenced, but it cannot be copied directly.

## AI Is Becoming a New Variable

The two parties spent considerable time discussing AI during the seminar.

Bigo mentioned a change that is currently taking place: AI tools are lowering the threshold for content production. In the past, a streamer needed editing, operations, and subtitles to be handled separately. Now, one person with several AI tools can complete these tasks.

The research team of PT Metro Timur Indonusa summarized several directions in which AI is changing game live streaming:

Reducing content production costs. AI can automatically edit highlight clips, generate multilingual subtitles, and produce content summaries. A streamer can produce far more content than before.

Supplementing non-prime-time periods. During late-night hours, the number of human streamers decreases, and AI virtual streamers can fill the gap. Bigo has already tested the late-night live-streaming performance of AI streamers in some markets.

Improving interactive experience. More intelligent bullet-screen interactions, real-time translation, and personalized recommendations are all areas where AI is already being applied.

However, PT Metro Timur Indonusa pointed out during the seminar that AI will not replace top streamers, but it will replace streamers who “only know how to play games but do not know how to create content.”

This is because what AI can solve is an efficiency problem — editing, translation, and distribution. But it cannot solve the question of “why viewers should watch you.” Viewers come because they like the person, not because the picture quality is clear or the subtitles are complete.

## Three Observations

Based on this seminar, PT Metro Timur Indonusa summarized three judgments:

First, competition is shifting from “traffic” to “retention.” Signing fees can only bring people in; keeping them requires a complete set of elements — stable income expectations, a growth path, and a sense of being valued.

Second, AI is a tool, not the answer. AI can help streamers create more content, but whether streamers can survive still depends on whether platforms can help them earn money steadily. The income of mid-tier streamers in Indonesia is highly unstable, and AI cannot solve this problem either.

Third, do not copy any market directly. Bringing the Twitch model from the United States to Indonesia will most likely not work. Payment methods are different, willingness to pay is different, and network conditions are different. Whoever understands these differences first will avoid unnecessary detours.

Finally

Before the seminar concluded, Bigo shared one detail: it recently held several offline gatherings for game streamers in Indonesia, and attendance was twice as high as expected.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes this signal deserves attention. Streamers want to know that platforms do not merely regard them as traffic entry points, but as long-term partners.

AI is a new variable, but the fundamental question has not changed: why should streamers stay here?

Whoever can answer this question well will be able to remain in the second half of game live streaming.
