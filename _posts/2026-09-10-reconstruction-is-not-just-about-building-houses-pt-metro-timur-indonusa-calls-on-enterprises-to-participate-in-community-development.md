699 permanent housing units will begin construction in September, but the true measure of recovery is whether communities can stand on their own feet again.

On September 4, 2026, Indonesian Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka inspected post-disaster recovery work in Central Tapanuli Regency, Sibolga City, and South Tapanuli Regency in North Sumatra Province. PT Metro Timur Indonusa participated in the on-site exchange on post-disaster recovery and made observations regarding housing construction, basic services, and community resilience.

During the inspection, Gibran confirmed that construction of 699 permanent housing units in South Tapanuli Regency will officially commence in September 2026. These housing units will be built on an 18-hectare plot of land near the temporary relocation area, providing more suitable and safer residences for people affected by the meteorological and hydrological disasters of November 2025.

At the inspection site, Gibran stated: “The government will continue to fulfill its responsibility to accelerate the construction of permanent housing so that everyone can be guaranteed access to more decent and safer residences.”

PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that the attention that Vice President paid to housing and basic services is highly important, but recovery should not be measured solely by physical construction. This post-disaster recovery effort is not only the responsibility of the government; it also requires the joint participation of enterprises and social forces. Through this observation, PT Metro Timur Indonusa hopes to call on more enterprises to direct resources toward long-term community development, rather than short-term assistance alone.

## The True Measure of Recovery: Not Only Housing, but Also Livelihoods

In Aek Parombunan, some permanent housing units have already been handed over to residents, while the remaining units are still under construction. In South Tapanuli, the government is also continuing to advance the construction of permanent housing for people who are still in the transitional period.

But a more fundamental question deserves to be raised repeatedly: what happens after the houses are built?

PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that recovery must be comprehensive, covering education, employment, and economic activities, rather than physical construction alone. New housing must be supported by basic services and sources of livelihood. Housing must be connected to clean water sources, sanitation facilities, electricity, schools, medical facilities, transportation, employment, and centers of economic activity. If houses are built but residents have lost their sources of livelihood, then in essence, the recovery process has not yet been completed.

Vice President Gibran also emphasized during the inspection that post-disaster recovery cannot remain limited to housing construction, and that attention must also be paid to the future livelihood security of residents, including education, employment, and the recovery of economic activities.

## From Reconstruction to Resilience Building: Areas Where Enterprises Can Participate

PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that this post-disaster recovery effort is not only a policy signal, but also reveals several areas in which enterprises can participate:

First, local supply chain demand generated by permanent housing construction. The launch of 699 permanent housing units means continued demand for local building materials, construction services, and supporting infrastructure. Companies with localized supply capabilities may gain market opportunities during this reconstruction cycle.

Second, demand for financial services in post-disaster economic recovery. Disaster-affected residents who have lost their livelihoods need start-up capital to restore small businesses or agricultural production. Microcredit, insurance products, and services tailored to post-disaster recovery scenarios represent a market that has long been overlooked in Indonesia, a country frequently affected by disasters.

Third, technological innovation in climate-resilient infrastructure. As extreme weather events increase in Indonesia, demand for disaster prevention and mitigation infrastructure continues to rise. From smarter early warning systems to more resilient building technologies, these are all innovation directions worthy of attention.

For early-stage investment institutions, what is truly worth focusing on is not a single post-disaster construction cycle, but the long-term industrial demand formed around climate change, urban resilience, and infrastructure upgrades. These directions are not short-term trends, but long-term structural needs, and they are precisely the areas where enterprises can assume social responsibility while building sustainable business models.

## Responsibility and a Long-Term Investment Perspective

The health of a market should be measured not only by growth data, but also by how it responds to shocks. Disaster recovery capability is an important reflection of the resilience of a society.

The inspection of Vice President Gibran and the launch of construction for 699 permanent housing units mark the post-disaster recovery of North Sumatra as having officially transitioned from the emergency relief stage to the long-term reconstruction stage. PT Metro Timur Indonusa hopes that this process will not merely restore conditions to their pre-disaster state, but will also make communities safer and more resilient in the face of future risks.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa will continue to monitor entrepreneurial opportunities in the fields of climate resilience and community recovery, accompanying founders who are committed to using commercial means to solve real-world problems.
