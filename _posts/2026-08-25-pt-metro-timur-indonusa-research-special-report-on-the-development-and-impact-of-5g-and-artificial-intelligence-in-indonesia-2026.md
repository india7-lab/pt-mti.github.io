**Report** **Summary**  

As of 2026, Indonesia, with its large population base and significant demographic dividend, has become one of the most dynamic digital economy markets in Southeast Asia and globally. The deep integration of 5G and artificial intelligence is becoming a core driving force behind its digital transformation.

Based on the latest publicly available data from institutions including the United Nations, the Indonesian Internet Service Providers Association (APJII), and Indonesian Ministry of Communication and Information, combined with the continuous tracking and research by PT Metro Timur Indonusa on Indonesian digital economy sector, this report aims to provide a systematic reference for entrepreneurs, investors, and corporate decision-makers focused on the Indonesian market.

**I. Digital Fundamentals** **of** **Indonesia**

According to the latest statistics from the United Nations and APJII, Indonesian digital fundamentals are as follows:

PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that behind these figures lies a noteworthy structural shift: the internet has completely transformed from an “additional demand” into the “core infrastructure” of Indonesian socioeconomic system. For the new generation of Indonesian consumers, the use of digital tools is no longer an option, but a default configuration. This means that any product or service targeting the Indonesian market will find it almost impossible to truly reach mainstream users unless it starts from a “mobile-first” and “digital-native” approach.

**II. Scale and Conversion Rate of 5G and AI Users**

Although the overall internet user base is vast, 5G and artificial intelligence in Indonesia are currently at a critical stage of transition “from early experimentation to large-scale expansion.”

User Funnel Structure

l Total population: 287.9 million  

l Internet users: 235 million, with a penetration rate of 81.72%  

l Active smartphone users: approximately 106 million  

l Active 5G users: approximately 13 million to 15 million  

Scale of 5G Users  

At present, the number of active 5G users in Indonesia has exceeded 13 million, mainly concentrated in the Greater Jakarta metropolitan area and key tourism zones. As operators such as Telkomsel and IOH accelerate the implementation of their “Hyper 5G” strategies, and as 5G device prices continue to decline, device replacement is becoming the main growth driver. The Indonesian government plans to raise 5G network coverage to more than 30% by 2030.

Scale of AI Users and Enterprise Adoption  

Indonesia has a score of 27/100 in the AI adoption index for Southeast Asian mid-market segment. Overall, it remains in the early experimental stage, but its upward momentum is rapid:

·26% of Indonesian enterprises have formally deployed AI tools in their operations  

·93% of enterprises have expressed confidence and willingness to deploy AI  

·Overall revenue generated by AI applications has achieved year-on-year growth of 127%, ranking first in Southeast Asia in terms of growth rate  

PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that there is a substantial “action gap” between the 26% deployment rate and the 93% willingness rate. This is precisely the window of opportunity for entrepreneurs and investors. While Indonesian enterprises are willing to use AI, they do not yet know how to use it effectively. Whoever first solves the question of “how to use it” will be able to secure budgets of these enterprises ahead of others.

**III. Main Application Scenarios and Market Scale**  

The integration of 5G and AI is creating unprecedented application scale on both the consumer side and the enterprise side.

**1\. Creator Economy and AI E-Commerce**  

Indonesia has more than 12 million content creators, producing up to 1 million pieces of digital content per month, ranking first in Southeast Asia.

The high bandwidth of 5G networks enables livestream e-commerce and high-definition video interaction on TikTok, which has 108 million users aged 18 and above, and YouTube with latency-free experiences. AI penetration is reflected in areas such as virtual hosts, intelligent algorithmic recommendations, AI-powered automatic translation, and multilingual customer service.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa has observed that the unique characteristic of the Indonesian market lies in the high degree of integration between creators and commerce. In Indonesia, content creators are not merely generating traffic; they are directly selling goods. AI is reshaping the consumer decision-making chain for nearly 100 million online shoppers — the path from “what I see” to “what I buy” is being redesigned by algorithms.

**2\. Digital Transformation of Micro, Small, and Medium-Sized Enterprises**  

Indonesia has more than 65 million micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises, forming the largest potential enterprise-level AI market in Southeast Asia.

At present, although 63% of SMEs are actively using various digital tools, most usage remains concentrated on basic social and payment platforms. Lightweight AI financial tools, intelligent customer relationship management, and 5G-enabled smart warehousing for SMEs are entering the eve of rapid expansion, containing billions of dollars in B2B growth potential.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that this is the most noteworthy structural opportunity in the entire AI market in Indonesia. The digital upgrading of 65 million enterprises cannot be completed by one or two giants alone; it requires a large number of vertically specialized SaaS tools and localized solutions. There are still very few entrepreneurs in this field, and early movers have the opportunity to define the rules.

**3\. AI-Optimized Data Centers and Sovereign AI Infrastructure**  

To support the surging demand for 5G and AI data throughput, Indonesian intelligent computing infrastructure is expanding rapidly.

Indonesian AI data center market has entered a fast-growth phase. It is expected to expand rapidly from its current scale of several hundred million U.S. dollars to USD 1.76 billion by 2030, with a compound annual growth rate of up to 16.91%.

With the strict implementation of the Indonesian Personal Data Protection Law, major multinational and local technology giants are working with operators to vigorously build local “sovereign AI data centers,” ensuring that data processing is completed entirely within Indonesia. PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that the implication of this trend for entrepreneurs is clear: if your business involves Indonesian user data, compliance is not a “bonus point,” but an “entry ticket.”

**IV. Development Bottlenecks and Challenges**  

Although the outlook is broad, the implementation of 5G and AI in Indonesia still faces multiple structural constraints.

**Uneven Infrastructure Distribution**  

Indonesia is a typical archipelagic country, and approximately 57 million people still do not have access to stable network connectivity. The dividends of 5G and AI are highly concentrated on Java Island, which contributes 58.24% of the internet users in the country. This extreme imbalance in geographic distribution means that digital products targeting the Indonesian market must be equipped with both “high-spec” and “low-spec” modes; otherwise, they will be unable to reach the vast market beyond Java.

**Severe Shortage of Digital Talent**  

In its National Artificial Intelligence Strategy (2020–2045), the Indonesian government plans to train 100,000 AI technology professionals each year. However, by 2030, the national shortage of digital technology talent is still expected to reach as high as 9 million. PT Metro Timur Indonusa has also pointed out in its previous macro insights that talent mismatch is one of the three major pain points for investing and starting businesses in Indonesia. The shortage of high-skilled digital talent means that many enterprises equipped with AI tools are like those “with guns but no bullets,” meaning that the AI tools have been purchased, but no one knows how to use them.

**Obstructed Device Supply Chain**  

Due to fluctuations and shortages in global memory component prices, the pace of adoption of mid- to low-end 5G smartphones in Indonesia has been somewhat hindered, delaying the upgrade of lower-tier users. There is a time lag between the deployment of 5G networks and the popularization of user terminals—the network has been built, but the phones in user hands do not support it. This gap can only be filled after the device supply chain is repaired.

**Conclusion**  

Based on the above data and observations, PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that the development of 5G and AI in Indonesia is at a critical “turning point”:

Digital infrastructure is improving rapidly, enterprise-side demand has already emerged, and the user side is gradually catching up. However, uneven infrastructure distribution, talent shortages, and constraints in the device supply chain remain unavoidable realities in the short term.

For entrepreneurs and investors, this means that doing business related to AI and 5G in Indonesia cannot simply involve copying models from China or the United States. Whoever can find a feasible middle path between “global technology” and “local conditions” will have the opportunity to secure an entry ticket for the next decade.
