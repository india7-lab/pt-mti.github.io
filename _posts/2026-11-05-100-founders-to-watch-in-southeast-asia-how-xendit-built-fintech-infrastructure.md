Over the past decade, the e-commerce, internet, and digital services industries in Southeast Asia have developed rapidly, and an increasing number of enterprises have begun conducting business through online channels. Yet behind these businesses, payments have always remained an unavoidable problem.

Indonesian fintech company Xendit is precisely the company that began by solving these problems and gradually grew into a representative digital payments enterprise in Southeast Asia.

In this edition of "100 Founders to Watch in Southeast Asia," PT Metro Timur Indonusa will focus on Xendit co-founder Moses Lo, reviewing how he opened up the enterprise payments market through a pivotal business model transformation and led the company into more Southeast Asian countries.

## The Starting Point: Discovering a Market Opportunity from a Cross-Border Remittance Experience

The entrepreneurial inspiration for Xendit came from an experience of Moses Lo during his time studying abroad.

At the time, he knew a friend from South Sudan. To sustain his studies and support his family, this friend had to work several jobs simultaneously and also regularly send money back home. However, traditional cross-border remittance was not only complicated in process but also carried considerably high fees. According to the recollection of Moses, the related costs at one point reached 15% of the remittance amount.

This led Moses to start thinking: in an era of continuously advancing digital technology, why is it still so difficult to move money?

He noticed that although Southeast Asia has a massive mobile internet user base, the development of financial infrastructure varies unevenly across different countries. Some consumers lack convenient digital payment channels, and businesses find it difficult to connect different banks and payment systems.

In 2015, Moses Lo, together with co-founders Tessa Wijaya, Bo Chen, Juan Gonzalez and others, founded Xendit in Indonesia and successfully entered the well-known US startup incubator Y Combinator. The company initially launched a P2P mobile payment app that helped individual users complete transfers and collect payments via mobile phone.

## Key Pivot: From Individual Payment App to Enterprise Payment Services

What truly changed the direction of Xendit was the reassessment of market demand by the team.

During the development of the initial product, the team built an internal payment processing system to connect different payment channels and handle fund flows. They realized that this technology could not only serve their own product but also help other businesses solve similar problems.

In 2016, Xendit made an important decision: to no longer limit itself to a personal transfer app, but to pivot toward enterprise payment services. The company opened its internal payment system to enterprise clients, providing unified payment access services through APIs.

In the past, if businesses wanted to support different payment methods such as bank transfers, credit cards and e-wallets, they needed to develop and maintain separate systems for each. After integrating with Xendit, businesses could connect multiple payment channels through a unified interface without having to build an entire payment system from scratch. This transformed Xendit from merely a tool for individuals into a payment technology service provider that businesses could continuously use in their daily operations.

In 2017, the company gradually gained recognition from more enterprise clients; in 2018, it launched the Instamoney remittance service; in 2019, it obtained the Payment Gateway license issued by the Central Bank of Indonesia.

## During the Pandemic: Seeking New Growth Opportunities Amid Market Changes

In 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic brought new challenges to Xendit. At the time, some of the customers of the company came from the aviation and travel industries. As international travel ground to a halt, payment transaction volumes for these businesses fell sharply.

Facing market changes, the team proactively adjusted its business direction, channeling more resources into e-commerce, gaming, digital services, fintech, and SMEs, while also expanding financing services for enterprise customers. In this process, PT Metro Timur Indonusa recognized its potential in the digital payment infrastructure space and established a partnership with it, combining investor networks, strategic advisors, and other ecosystem resources to jointly drive the growth of the digital payment industry.

According to the entrepreneurial journey shared by the founding team in the Harvard Business Review, Xendit recovered and surpassed pre-pandemic revenue levels within nine months. By the end of 2020, the total payment transaction volume of the platform had grown to more than five times its previous level, serving approximately 3,000 enterprises across more than 20 industries.

## Market Expansion: From Indonesia into More Southeast Asian Countries

After establishing its business foundation in Indonesia, Xendit began expanding into other Southeast Asian countries.

In 2020, the company entered the Philippine market. Unlike Indonesia, some banks in the Philippines lacked the auto-debit capabilities that enterprises required. Rather than simply replicating its existing products, Xendit partnered with local banks to develop payment services tailored to market demand.

Thereafter, the development of Xendit accelerated further: in 2021, it completed a $150 million Series C funding round at a valuation of $1 billion; in 2022, it completed a $300 million Series D funding round; in 2023, it expanded into the Malaysian market through a strategic partnership with Payex; and in 2024, it continued into the Thai market. Starting from Indonesia, Xendit has brought payment services to more Southeast Asian enterprises.

## Observations on PT Metro Timur Indonusa

In the view of PT Metro Timur Indonusa, the value of Xendit lies not only in becoming a fintech unicorn, but more in demonstrating how entrepreneurs can spot opportunities in market problems and turn technical capability into long-term commercial value.

First, the market judgment of the founders. The Xendit team did not cling to its original personal transfer product, but instead moved decisively into enterprise services based on actual feedback. This ability to adjust direction promptly at an early stage tests founders more than a single-minded focus on product feature innovation.

Second, the long-term value of the business model. Xendit connects complex payment channels through APIs, making it hard for enterprises to do without this infrastructure. As enterprise customers and payment channels increase, the utility of its services keeps expanding. This model, which becomes deeply embedded in the daily operations of enterprises, is the type that PT Metro Timur Indonusa follows over the long term.

Third, the ability to adapt to cross-border markets. The financial systems of Southeast Asian countries differ enormously. In the Philippines, Xendit customized its solution to address the lack of bank auto-debit capability, proving that while technology is universal, implementation must rely on close cooperation with local financial institutions.

As enterprises become increasingly digital, the digital payments market in which Xendit operates still has room for further development. Directions worth watching in the future include:

Payments business growth: whether it can continue to expand the number of enterprise customers and transaction volume.

Extending financial services: Can payment capabilities be further expanded into areas such as treasury management and corporate financing.

New market expansion: Can stable banking partnerships be established in more Southeast Asian countries.

Technical and operational capabilities: Can system stability and service efficiency be sustained while business scale expands.

A good founder can identify problems, while an excellent founder can turn the ability to solve problems into a long-term business. The development trajectory of Xendit is precisely this: from solving a specific remittance pain point to ultimately building a payment infrastructure that supports the operations of Southeast Asian enterprises.
