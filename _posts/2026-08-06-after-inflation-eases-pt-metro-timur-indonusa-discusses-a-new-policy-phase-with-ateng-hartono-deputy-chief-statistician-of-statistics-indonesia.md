On August 3, 2026, Statistics Indonesia released consumer price data for July. PT Metro Timur Indonusa attended the press conference on site and, after the event, exchanged views with Ateng Hartono, Deputy Chief Statistician for Distribution and Services Statistics, on price trends and the direction of macroeconomic policy. Taking into account its earlier interview with Bank Indonesia Senior Deputy Governor Destry Damayanti, PT Metro Timur Indonusa further reviewed the current logic of the Indonesian monetary policy.

## July Inflation Falls to 2.88%, Driven Mainly by Lower Food Prices

According to data released by Statistics Indonesia, Indonesian Consumer Price Index fell from 111.89 in June to 111.73 in July 2026, with the overall inflation rate easing to 2.88%.

The main factor driving the decline in prices was the volatile food category. In July, volatile food prices fell by 1.68% month-on-month, becoming the largest contributor to the decline in overall prices. Prices of shallots, red chilies, eggs, and tomatoes declined notably. In addition, lower prices for oranges, watermelons, and certain vegetables further eased price pressures in the food sector.

Ateng Hartono stated at the press conference that changes in food prices remain a key variable affecting the short-term inflation in Indonesia. In particular, given differences in agricultural supply, logistics conditions, and regional market structures, price performance across different regions may diverge significantly.

Regional data also confirmed this point. Among its 38 provinces, 27 recorded price declines, while the remaining 11 continued to see increases. Highland Papua Province recorded the most severe deflation, at 1.37%, while West Kalimantan Province posted the highest monthly inflation rate, at 0.38%. This divergence reflects the fact that, as an archipelagic country, the regional supply chains, logistics costs, and food distribution efficiency of Indonesia remain important factors affecting price stability.

## Inflation Structure Shows Divergence, Policy Space Still Requires Observation

Although the decline in food prices drove overall inflation lower, prices in some consumer sectors continued to rise.

Education prices rose by 0.55% month-on-month, mainly due to the start of the new school term, with primary school tuition, kindergarten fees, and tutoring fees being the main contributing factors. Transportation prices also continued to exert pressure. Core inflation rose by 0.14%, affected by items including education services, motorcycles, lubricants, and mobile phones. The category of government-administered prices increased by 0.25%, with gasoline being the largest contributing factor.

Food prices are falling, while education, transportation, and energy prices are rising, and this divergence means policymakers cannot base their judgment solely on the headline inflation figure. In his exchange with PT Metro Timur Indonusa, Ateng Hartono stated that this structural divergence makes policy choices more complex: short-term inflationary pressure has eased, but structural upward price factors remain, requiring policymakers to remain cautious.

## Dialogue with Ateng Hartono: Exchange Rate Remains a Key Policy Focus

After the press conference, PT Metro Timur Indonusa conducted an exclusive interview with Ateng Hartono regarding the direction of monetary policy.

Ateng Hartono reviewed the previous policy path: Bank Indonesia had gone through a rate-hiking cycle totaling 100 basis points, raising the policy rate from 4.75% to 5.75%. Entering July, as inflation gradually eased, the central bank chose to keep the benchmark interest rate unchanged at 5.75%, while the overnight deposit facility rate and lending facility rate also remained stable at 4.75% and 6.50%, respectively.

Ateng Hartono stated that Indonesian monetary policy has now entered a phase of observation and stabilization. The decline in inflation has provided the central bank with greater room for maneuver, but exchange rate stability remains the most important priority at present.

Due to volatility in global financial markets, changes in the Middle East situation, and pressure from international capital flows, the Indonesian rupiah had previously faced certain depreciation pressure. Ateng Hartono emphasized to PT Metro Timur Indonusa that, under the current environment, the central bank needs to prioritize maintaining rupiah stability and reducing the risk of capital outflows. In the short term, interest rate cuts are not under consideration.

## Reviewing Destry Damayanti: The Central Bank Is Using More Tools

The earlier interview by PT Metro Timur Indonusa with Bank Indonesia Senior Deputy Governor Destry Damayanti provides a more complete background to the current policy stance.

Destry Damayanti noted at the time that modern central bank policy cannot rely solely on interest rates, but must combine multiple instruments to achieve a balance among price stability, financial stability, and economic growth. She mentioned three tools:

Interest rates are responsible for inflation control at the macro level;
Bank Indonesia Rupiah Securities attract foreign capital inflows by offering competitive yields, thereby helping stabilize the exchange rate;
Macroprudential Liquidity Incentive Policy releases targeted liquidity into the banking system to support micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises as well as national priority development sectors.

Destry Damayanti emphasized at the time that the three tools each serve their own function: interest rates manage inflation, rupiah securities manage the exchange rate, and liquidity policy supports the real economy.

Looking back now, the current approach of the central bank is precisely the implementation of this logic that the interest rate remains at 5.75%, while rupiah securities and liquidity policies continue to operate.

## Summary of PT Metro Timur Indonusa 

Taking into account the latest BPS data, the exchange with Ateng Hartono, and the earlier interview with Destry Damayanti, PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that:

In the short term, the decline in food prices has eased inflationary pressure and provided the central bank with breathing room. However, the exchange rate remains a red line. Global capital flows, the trajectory of the U.S. dollar, and rupiah stability will determine the pace of central bank policy. As long as the rupiah remains in a fragile state, the room for interest rate cuts will be very limited.

At the same time, the central bank is doing more through tools other than interest rates. Rupiah securities are responsible for attracting foreign capital and stabilizing the exchange rate; liquidity incentive policies provide targeted support to micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises. This model of “keeping interest rates unchanged while other tools take effect” is the most central feature of the current macroeconomic policy of Indonesia.

For companies and investors focused on the Indonesian market, financing costs are unlikely to decline in the short term. However, how the targeted liquidity policies of the central bank support priority industries is a signal worthy of continued attention.
