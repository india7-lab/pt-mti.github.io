At 5:58 a.m. on August 15, 2026, a magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck the waters north of Flores Island in the East Nusa Tenggara Province in Indonesia.

As of August 19, the earthquake had resulted in 70 fatalities, injured more than 1,100 people, damaged over 4,500 houses, and affected nearly 20,000 people. Following the main shock, the region recorded more than 2,700 aftershocks, including more than 20 perceptible aftershocks of magnitude 5 or above.

After the disaster, the Indonesian government, rescue agencies, and local enterprises quickly activated emergency response mechanisms. PT Metro Timur Indonusa also participated in this community relief effort, working with local enterprises and community partners to support post-disaster recovery.

## Multiple Challenges Brought by a Shallow Offshore Earthquake

The earthquake occurred in the complex tectonic plate boundary region in Indonesia and was a typical shallow offshore strong earthquake. Decades of accumulated stress along the Flores-Wetar back-arc thrust fault zone were released in an instant.

Because the hypocenter was shallow, the Meteorology, Climatology, and Geophysical Agency in Indonesia quickly issued tsunami warnings for 22 surrounding coastal areas. Ende Regency recorded a tsunami wave amplitude of approximately 0.94 meters, while Labuan Bajo recorded a 30-centimeter change, and some coastal villages experienced seawater backflow.

The more devastating impact came from the aftershock sequence. Within 40 minutes after the main shock, three strong aftershocks of around magnitude 6 occurred in succession. Ruteng Airport was severely damaged, the ceiling of Maumere Port collapsed, landslides blocked multiple roads, and several towns near the epicenter were temporarily cut off from the outside world.

During the Independence Day ceremony on August 17, President Prabowo led the nation in a moment of silence. The government declared a 14-day state of emergency in East Nusa Tenggara Province and deployed more than 1,800 military and police personnel to participate in rescue operations.

## Local Enterprises Respond Urgently, Forming a Community Relief Network  

In the face of the sudden disaster, a number of Indonesian local enterprises and institutions quickly launched relief operations:

Fastpay used its payment and logistics network to coordinate relief resources and provide daily necessities to affected areas. Its relief network has covered 20 temporary shelters in East Nusa Tenggara Province.

PNM conducted an emergency assessment of micro, small, and medium-sized enterprise clients and employees in the affected areas, and urgently allocated daily necessities for delivery to Flores Island.

Rans Entertainment, together with the government, launched an online public fundraising campaign, using its social media influence to raise reconstruction funds for the disaster-stricken areas.

Kompas Media Group connected social resources through its humanitarian fund and delivered supplies to areas where displaced residents were concentrated, including Maumere Stadium.

These actions reflect the important role of Indonesian local enterprises in participating in social support through their own networks during emergencies.

## Post-Disaster Recovery Is Still Ongoing  

As of August 19, the disaster-stricken areas were still facing challenges including continuing aftershocks, drinking water supply, medical resources, and infrastructure restoration. Official estimates indicate that aftershock activity may continue for another three to four weeks, while nearly 20,000 displaced residents are still spending the night outdoors, in stadiums, or in tents.

For enterprises and investors that have long focused on the Indonesian market, this disaster once again demonstrates that, as an archipelagic country located along the Pacific Ring of Fire, the Indonesian logistics resilience, disaster response capacity, and the effectiveness of community networks are fundamental issues that any institution seeking long-term roots in the country must take seriously.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa will continue to monitor the progress of post-disaster recovery in Flores and coordinate subsequent support actions according to actual conditions.
