High-end projects are holding up the market, while mid- to low-end segments remain weak, and the dual pressure from interest rates and the exchange rate has yet to ease.

In the first half of 2026, the performance of Indonesian listed real estate companies remained broadly weak. After reviewing the latest financial report data from several leading developers, PT Metro Timur Indonusa found that most real estate companies were still experiencing negative growth in revenue and profit, and that the industry adjustment has not yet reached a bottom.

In a recent exchange between PT Metro Timur Indonusa and Ateng Hartono, Deputy Head of Statistics Indonesia, Hartono also mentioned one detail: although overall inflation has declined somewhat, driven by lower food prices, changes in the real estate market have not been fully incorporated into the CPI structure. In reality, weak demand and cost pressures in this sector are an important reason why the “felt temperature” of Indonesian economy is lower than what the macro data suggests.

## Ciputra Development: Revenue and Profit Both Decline, High-End Projects Become the Only Support  

PT Metro Timur Indonusa first focused its attention on established developer PT Ciputra Development Tbk (CTRA) in Indonesia.

As of the end of June 2026, the operating revenue of CTRA was IDR 5.19 trillion, down 11.69% year on year from IDR 5.88 trillion in the same period last year. Affected by the decline in revenue, the CTRA net profit at the end of the first half of 2026 fell sharply by 11.19% year on year to IDR 1.09 trillion.

What is even more noteworthy is the marketing sales data. In the first quarter of 2026, CTRA recorded presales of IDR 2.44 trillion, completing only 26% of its full-year target. Cumulative marketing sales in the first half reached IDR 4.7 trillion, down 18% year on year.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa noted that the CTRA sales structure shows a clear “K-shaped divergence,” with its sales focus heavily dependent on high-end residential properties priced above IDR 1.5 billion per unit, and most transactions completed with the help of the government VAT exemption policy. This means that the lack of purchasing power in the mid- to low-end market is becoming the core factor dragging down performance. While the high-end market still provides support, its high dependence on policy raises questions about sustainability.

Looking back at the full year of 2025, CTRA recorded revenue of IDR 12.6 trillion and net profit of IDR 2.66 trillion. Based on the trend in the first half of the year, PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that the full-year profit of CTRA in 2026 is expected to decline by around 10%, a judgment broadly consistent with previous industry expectations.

## Peer Comparison: BSDE Holds the Line, PANI Grows Against the Trend  

PT Metro Timur Indonusa also reviewed the performance of two other major developers to provide a reference point for its industry assessment.

Bumi Serpong Damai (BSDE) has maintained its 2026 marketing sales target at IDR 10 trillion, basically unchanged from 2025, which was approximately IDR 10.04 trillion. The confidence of BSDE mainly comes from mature township projects in the Greater Jakarta area and land parcels near transportation hubs. PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that the BSDE strategy is to “defend its position”—not seeking growth, but preserving core assets and cash flow.

Pantai Indah Kapuk Dua (PANI), however, has delivered an independent upward trend. Benefiting from the continued advancement of the PIK2 coastal new city project and the accelerated implementation of supporting infrastructure, PANI achieved marketing sales of IDR 1.9 trillion in the first half of 2026, up 66% year on year. Its net profit in the first half surged to IDR 1.7 trillion, even exceeding the CTRA net profit during the same period.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa judges that the counter-trend growth of PANI does not mean that the industry as a whole is recovering, but rather reflects the concentrated release of regional and project-specific dividends. As a scarce large-scale coastal development project in North Jakarta, PIK2, together with infrastructure expectations, has created a short-term supply-demand mismatch.

## Three Core Reasons Behind the Industry Slump  

PT Metro Timur Indonusa attributes the current downturn in the real estate sector to three levels:

First, the exhaustion of mid- to low-end purchasing power. This is the most fundamental reason. Demand for residential properties priced below IDR 1 billion continues to shrink, and mass-market owner-occupier buyers have been most directly affected by the slowdown in overall economic circulation. The earlier interview of PT Metro Timur Indonusa with Destry Damayanti also confirmed the observation that the liquidity released by the central bank through its KLM policy has mainly flowed to micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises and priority development sectors, rather than to individual housing credit.

Second, rupiah depreciation and cost pressure. The weakening of the Indonesian rupiah against the U.S. dollar has directly pushed up the cost of imported building materials, such as steel, aluminum, and certain finishing materials, as well as transportation costs, continuously compressing the profit margins of developers. Ateng Hartono also mentioned at the July inflation press conference that although food prices had fallen, inflationary pressure transmitted through imports still required caution.

Third, financial conditions and prudent credit standards. Bank Indonesia has maintained its policy rate at 5.75%, and banks have become more stringent in approving mortgage loans, especially with a notable tightening in buyer credit for mid- to low-priced housing. PT Metro Timur Indonusa also mentioned in its exchange with Ateng Hartono that there is limited room for rate cuts in the short term, which means the financing costs of homebuyers are unlikely to decline in the near term.

## Summary from PT Metro Timur Indonusa

Based on the financial report data of CTRA, BSDE, and PANI, as well as the macro policy environment, PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that:

Indonesian real estate market is currently in a phase of divergence, with “high-end resilience and mid- to low-end bottom-searching.” High interest rates and depreciation pressure on the rupiah have limited the room for policy easing, while the reliance of developers on high-end products and specific regions can support performance in the short term but is unlikely to reverse the overall downward trend of the industry.

For investors following Indonesian real estate sector, the two core variables in the current market remain: first, whether the rupiah exchange rate can stabilize; and second, whether the central bank has room to send a rate-cut signal at the end of the year or early next year. Before these two conditions change substantially, an overall recovery in the real estate sector will still take time.
