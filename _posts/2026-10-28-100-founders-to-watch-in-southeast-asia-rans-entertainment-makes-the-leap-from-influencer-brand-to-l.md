Turning personal fame into a lasting business is often harder than building a new brand from scratch.

For early-stage entrepreneurs, personal talent or recognition can usually attract an initial wave of attention. The real test, however, is turning a personality-driven operation into an institutionalized company that can keep running and withstand risks before the traffic boom fades.

In Indonesia, celebrity couple Raffi Ahmad and Nagita Slavina, both household names, have achieved a textbook transformation. As part of the “100 Founders to Watch in Southeast Asia” series, this article from PT Metro Timur Indonusa focuses on the two founders of RANS Entertainment, examining how they spent more than a decade turning family videos shot with handheld cameras into a public company listed on the Indonesia Stock Exchange.

## The Limits of the Influencer Economy: Advertising Revenue Alone Can Only Take You So Far

Around 2015, social media was experiencing explosive growth worldwide. Indonesia was no exception, with large numbers of content creators joining platforms such as YouTube. At the time, however, most influencers relied on a single business model: produce videos, attract traffic, and earn a share of platform advertising revenue through AdSense.

This model was quick to launch, but its limitations were stark. Traffic depended entirely on platform algorithms, content was highly repetitive, and revenue could plunge once audiences lost interest. Although many celebrities and influencers accumulated millions of followers, they never established a genuine competitive moat and remained little more than “high-earning freelancers.”

PT Metro Timur Indonusa sees this problem—having traffic but lacking a business model—as a common challenge for early-stage content entrepreneurs. Those who are first to step outside their comfort zones and turn attention into products and real-world services can earn their place in the next stage of growth.

## Where the Founders Began: Family Videos in a Garage

In December 2015, Raffi Ahmad and Nagita Slavina launched a YouTube channel called RANS Entertainment. The name “RANS” was formed from the initials of their names. Their original idea was straightforward: as television celebrities, the couple wanted a space of their own where they could express themselves freely and document everyday family life.

The initial setup was extremely basic. They did not even have a formal office, working instead from their home garage. Apart from the couple, the initial team consisted of just three employees, using simple handheld cameras to film everyday makeup routines, parenting, and family trips.

Why did it work for them? Raffi Ahmad and Nagita Slavina had built strong public recognition through traditional television, along with an approachable quality that resonated deeply with Indonesian audiences. Their authentic family dynamic made them natural and unpretentious on camera, quickly attracting a large following. Within just a few years, their YouTube subscriber count reached tens of millions, while monthly advertising revenue ranged from hundreds of millions to billions of Indonesian rupiah.

But the founders did not stop at earning advertising revenue. Raffi Ahmad soon recognized that the enormous surge in audience attention needed to be treated as a serious business opportunity. They began operating the family-centered channel as a digital media company and assembled a creative young core team. Affectionately known to fans as the “PowerRANSger,” this group became instrumental in supporting the transition to professional operations.

## The Evolution of the Business Model: From Video Production to a Cross-Sector Ecosystem

What truly set RANS Entertainment apart was its remarkably ambitious expansion into traditional industries and lifestyle businesses.

In management, the couple developed a highly complementary partnership. Nagita Slavina served as Chief Executive Officer (CEO), overseeing disciplined, detail-oriented business operations, while Raffi Ahmad served as Chairman, drawing on his exceptional ability to bring together resources and relationships.

Leveraging their powerful personal brands, they began expanding into adjacent businesses. The platform launched RANS Music and RANS Sports, and even moved into professional sports through acquisitions and the establishment of football club RANS Nusantara FC and basketball team RANS PIK Basketball. They also entered film, television, and animation production, physical theme parks, consumer products, and investments in technology startups.

This was no longer a simple influencer channel, but a business network reaching across the lifestyles of young Indonesians.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa observes that the competitive moat of RANS lies in its successful transfer of fan trust in the founders to trust in the RANS brand. Buying sports teams and developing theme parks provided tangible assets through which online traffic could be converted into real-world business value. This approach strengthened corporate resilience and reduced dependence on rule changes at any single platform.

## Capital Backing: From Family Business to Public Company

From 2021 onward, the distinctive appeal of RANS attracted the attention of leading Indonesian investors.

In late 2021, Emtek Group, one of the largest traditional media and technology groups in Indonesia, invested IDR 248 billion in RANS through a subsidiary, providing substantial support for its offline events and content production. At the same time, the company brought prominent figures with political and business connections onto its board of commissioners, completing its transformation from a family business into a company with modern corporate governance.

Supported by strong financial performance, PT Rans Entertainmen Indonesia Tbk officially listed on the Indonesia Stock Exchange on July 10, 2026, under the ticker symbol RANS, raising IDR 429.25 billion.

This marked its full transition from a family-run influencer operation into a capital market participant subject to public scrutiny and supported by a formal financial reporting framework.

## The Influencer Program at PT Metro Timur Indonusa

PT Metro Timur Indonusa follows RANS Entertainment not because of the celebrity status of its founders, but because of the execution they demonstrated throughout their commercial transformation and their proof that “personal influence is itself a highly valuable asset.”

From an early-stage investment perspective, many founders with technical or product backgrounds tend to overlook the importance of communication and influence. They have strong projects but do not know how to tell their stories in a way the market can understand. The development of RANS offers a constructive example: establish trust first, then turn that trust into an ecosystem.

Recognizing the powerful contribution that personal brands can make to business growth, PT Metro Timur Indonusa previously launched its Influencer Program. The program is intended not only for established creators but also welcomes early-stage entrepreneurs with hands-on experience and industry insight. PT Metro Timur Indonusa seeks to use its capital network and industry resources to help founders turn their professional expertise into influence that gains market recognition.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa has always believed that the outstanding companies of the future will need to understand not only products but also communication. Founders who build personal influence through authentic communication are often better positioned to lead their companies through market cycles.

For early-stage entrepreneurs, RANS offers an important lesson: personal influence is the best source of initial leverage, but it must never be the final product. Building a lasting company requires converting attention, while it is at its peak, into tangible businesses, team structures, and assets recognized by the capital markets.
