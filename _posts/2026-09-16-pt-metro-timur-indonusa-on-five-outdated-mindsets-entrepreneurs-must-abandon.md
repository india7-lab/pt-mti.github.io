Many companies encounter bottlenecks not because the market has disappeared, but because their management criteria for judgment remain stuck three years in the past.

In the early stages of entrepreneurship, founders typically rely on personal judgment, execution capability, and a sharp sense of opportunity to move the company forward. However, as a business scales, its organizational complexity, capital structure, and external environment all change. If founders continue to apply the mindset of the early stage, they may instead become an obstacle to the company growth.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa has long tracked the growth trajectories of its portfolio companies and has repeatedly observed this phenomenon in its post-investment services. From the long-term perspective of an investment institution, an outstanding CEO is not the person with the strongest willpower or the strongest desire for control, but the one who can continuously update their cognition and decision-making approach.

This article summarizes five outdated mindsets that founders are most likely to fall into, and explains why they can become real obstacles once a company reaches a certain stage of growth.

## Outdated Mindset One: Treating the Personal Judgment of the Founder as the Most Reliable Source of Decision-Making

This is the most common problem and also the easiest to overlook.

When a company is small, information is concentrated in the hands of a few people, and founders can make decisions quickly and efficiently. But as the business expands and teams become more specialized, information becomes increasingly dispersed and professionalized. If the CEO insists on using personal experience to make all key decisions, the company cannot fully leverage the collective intelligence within the organization.

When evaluating a company, investment institutions focus on whether its decision-making mechanism is sustainable, rather than whether it depends on the judgment of a single individual. Truly mature managers must accept one fact: a systematic decision-making mechanism is often more stable than personal experience.

## Outdated Mindset Two: Believing That “Only Personal Involvement Can Guarantee Results”

Many founders are accustomed to being deeply involved in every detail, viewing this as a reflection of responsibility and efficiency. But when a company scales, this practice creates two problems:

On the one hand, the CEO is filled with operational decisions, leaving increasingly little room for long-term strategic thinking; on the other hand, team members lose the space to develop independent judgment and take responsibility. Over time, such an organization will find it difficult to build a stable management pipeline.

Investment institutions prefer to see a system that can operate automatically, rather than an organization that relies on a single point of support.

## Outdated Mindset Three: Placing Excessive Faith in the Product Itself

Many managers believe that as long as the product is good enough, the market will naturally respond. But in reality, the market does not automatically recognize value. User perceptions are influenced by information structures, trust relationships, and the efficiency of reach.

Even a technologically leading product will struggle to achieve scaled growth without clear messaging and reliable communication channels. Professional investment analysis distinguishes between product capability and market amplification capability—the latter often determines whether a company can cross a critical threshold.

CEOs need to understand that market communication is not a supporting function, but the process through which the value of a company is understood and accepted externally.

Outdated Mindset Four: Repeatedly Focusing on Short-Term Results

Revenue growth, periodic profits, and surface-level data can reflect operating conditions to some extent, but they are not equivalent to long-term value.

If management optimizes short-term metrics by cutting R&D investment, neglecting organizational development, or weakening user experience, the competitiveness of the company will be eroded without being noticed.

When assessing long-term potential, investment institutions pay more attention to whether a company is continuously strengthening its structural advantages, rather than its performance at a single point in time. Outstanding CEOs maintain clear judgment between short-term pressure and long-term objectives.

## Outdated Mindset Five: Relying on Past Paths to Success

A more hidden outdated mindset comes from reliance on past paths to success. When facing new problems, many companies are accustomed to looking back at methods that once worked and then attempting to replicate them.

However, market conditions, technology cycles, and user behavior are constantly changing, and past experience does not automatically translate into current success. If management continues to use historical cases to prove the rationality of current decisions, it may overlook the fact that key variables have already changed.

Truly completing the transition from founder to CEO requires the ability to remodel thinking in uncertain environments, acknowledge the boundaries of experience, and continuously update the basis for judgment.

## PT Metro Timur Indonusa Observations

Founders need to clearly recognize that these outdated mindsets share one common characteristic: they all treat the company as an extension of personal capability, rather than as a system that must constantly self-correct.

In its investment practice, PT Metro Timur Indonusa has observed that companies truly capable of moving through cycles are usually led by managers who are willing to continuously update their thinking. Their focus is not on proving that their past decisions were correct, but on ensuring that the company remains adaptable in the future.

The process of a founder becoming an outstanding CEO is, in essence, a continuous upgrade of cognition. Abandoning outdated mindsets is not a denial of past success, but a way to open new space for growth. From the perspective of long-term investment and business analysis, leaders who can keep evolving are the most reliable foundation of the company value.
