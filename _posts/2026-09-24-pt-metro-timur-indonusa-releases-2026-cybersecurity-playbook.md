## What Security Changes Startups Need to Watch in 2026

Disconnect between AI tool usage and data management. More and more employees are beginning to use personal AI tools in work scenarios, but enterprise data management and usage policies are often not established in tandem. The risk of core materials being mistakenly input into public-domain large models is becoming a real problem for many teams.

Governance gaps in automated agents. If automated AI agents deployed within enterprises lack effective governance, they may be exploited by external parties through low-code vulnerabilities or prompt injection to bypass traditional identity authentication. Agents have the ability to invoke systems and access data; once they go out of control, the scope of impact is far greater than that of an ordinary account breach.

Global data security requirements are escalating. In response to the risk that asymmetric encryption may become ineffective around 2030, startups going overseas or in hard tech have been forced to plan ahead for post-quantum cryptography starting from 2026.

## Phase One: Building Enterprise Digital Asset Management Awareness (From Week 1)

Move away from rigid password policies and prioritize governing the disorderly connections between "humans and AI."

Conduct a comprehensive inventory of AI assets. Use traffic analysis tools to survey and record all AI tools that touch company data. Clearly define the boundaries of AI usage. For teams using generative AI, it is recommended to procure enterprise-grade tools with data privacy isolation in a unified manner.

Modern identity governance. Clean up all "orphan accounts" of departed employees and former outsourced personnel. Not only must humans enable multi-factor authentication, but AI agents must also be brought into the audit scope. Configure dynamic token verification for APIs, automation scripts, and agent configurations across all internally connected systems.

A one-page emergency plan. Do not write lengthy compliance documents. The team must have a one-page emergency playbook: clearly define who is responsible for cutting off the network, who is responsible for notifying customers, and who is responsible for contacting cloud providers.

## Phase Two: Security Infrastructure for the Product Growth Stage (Before Product Launch)

In 2026, software supply chain attacks and API vulnerabilities are the hardest-hit areas for startups.

API and agent invocation protection. Strictly validate the input format of all external API interfaces to prevent malicious prompts from contaminating internal databases. It is strictly forbidden to hardcode any cloud provider keys or AI API keys in public code repositories such as GitHub.

Introduce adaptive endpoint protection. Startups typically do not have a 24-hour staffed security operations center. In 2026, priority should be given to endpoint protection tools with AI autonomous threat hunting and blocking capabilities, such as SentinelOne or CrowdStrike Falcon, to achieve automatic isolation and sandbox recovery when devices are attacked.

Test a backup recovery once. Having automated backups alone is not enough. Within this month, a real drill of "fully restoring the system from backup" must be conducted to ensure that backup files are not synchronously encrypted or corrupted when encountering new types of ransomware.

## Phase Three: Data Governance and Compliance Preparation in International Development (Business Expansion Period)

In 2026, global regulation of data sovereignty and AI trustworthiness has risen to an unprecedented height.

Automated compliance frameworks. If it is necessary to prove security capabilities to major clients or meet overseas regulatory requirements, do not manually compile materials. Directly use automated compliance platforms such as Vanta or Drata to continuously monitor cloud configurations 24 hours a day and automatically generate relevant compliance reports.

Reverse supply chain review. When procuring SaaS services or AI compute from upstream suppliers, it is necessary to counter-question: "If you encounter a security incident, how will our core assets be isolated?" Ensure that risk does not spread downward.

## Observations on PT Metro Timur Indonusa and MetroTimur-X

For early-stage startups, security building is not something to consider only after fundraising. Customer due diligence will ask about security issues, large enterprise procurement will require security compliance, and overseas business cannot avoid data sovereignty requirements. Security capability is shifting from a technical option to a commercial threshold.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa has observed in its post-investment services that teams which establish security awareness and foundational governance capabilities early on tend to move more smoothly through subsequent fundraising, key account expansion, and internationalization. This is not because their technology is stronger, but because they understood earlier what foundational capabilities long-term corporate development requires.

MetroTimur-X, as the startup research and knowledge empowerment platform under PT Metro Timur Indonusa, has long focused on the capability building of founders, and hopes that through this kind of action, it can guide them to break down complex security topics into executable steps, helping teams establish correct security habits early on.

This playbook does not demand that startups achieve perfection in the first week, but rather suggests advancing in stages: first establish awareness of digital asset management, then harden product and cloud security, and finally refine compliance and supply chain review. Each step does not require a massive budget, but it does require the attention of founders and sustained execution.
