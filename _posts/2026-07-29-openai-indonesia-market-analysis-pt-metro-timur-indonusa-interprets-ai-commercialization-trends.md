Indonesia has become one of the top five global markets by traffic for OpenAI, with more than 2 million conversations per day on average. PT Metro Timur Indonusa spoke with Aliisa Rosenthal, the early sales leader of OpenAI, to unpack the logic behind its tens-of-billions revenue growth and the reasons behind the Indonesian AI boom.

When Aliisa Rosenthal joined OpenAI, the company had almost no commercial activity.

It was a research laboratory. A group of top researchers were exploring the boundaries of technology, but terms such as product, sales, and customers were still far removed from them. Aliisa was one of the few early hires brought in to build the sales team. At the time, the entire sales team consisted of only four people.

Then ChatGPT was released.

Internally, the team had originally regarded it merely as a low-profile product experiment and had not expected it to trigger anything significant. What happened after the release was something no one had anticipated. ChatGPT became one of the fastest-growing products in the history of technology. Aliisa later recalled in a conversation with PT Metro Timur Indonusa that the internal state of OpenAI during that period could be described in one word: chaos.

It was not chaos in a negative sense, but rather that everyone had been pushed onto a completely unfamiliar high-speed track. Demand poured in from all directions. The team handled more than 10,000 customer leads every day and had to figure out, within an extremely short period of time, what enterprise customers actually wanted, how to charge them, and how to deliver.

In four years, the annual recurring revenue of OpenAI grew from approximately USD 10 million to USD 10 billion. The sales team expanded from 4 people to more than 300 people. Aliisa built the go-to-market engine of OpenAI from scratch and personally experienced the entire process of the company transformation from a nonprofit research institution into a global commercial AI giant.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa has long focused on how technology companies land and operate in Southeast Asia. There are many details in the OpenAI growth story worth examining closely. The more important question, however, is this: while OpenAI was surging globally, what happened in the Indonesian market? Why did Indonesia become one of its fastest-growing regions?

## I. How the Commercialization Engine of OpenAI Started Running

The personal experience of Aliisa provides a clear explanation of three sources of revenue for OpenAI:

Individual user subscriptions. ChatGPT Plus costs USD 20 per month and provides paying users with faster and more powerful models. This may look simple, but the product experience must be strong enough to persuade a large number of ordinary users to pay.

Enterprise customer services. ChatGPT Team and Enterprise versions are designed for corporate needs. They can connect to the company data, offer higher security standards, and integrate with workflows. Aliisa said that the growth rate of enterprise-side revenue was much faster than the team had expected. Among the 10,000 customer leads each day, a large portion came from companies asking how to deploy it for entire teams.

Developer usage-based payments. Startups and developers around the world call models through APIs and pay based on usage. OpenAI has effectively become an underlying technology provider in the AI era.

These three lines are not independent of one another. Individual users bring in enterprise customers, enterprise customers drive developers, and the products built by developers attract more individual users. This is a self-reinforcing loop that grows larger with every cycle.

In her conversation with PT Metro Timur Indonusa, Aliisa specifically mentioned one detail: during the most frenzied period of growth, the team was making decisions every day. Many of those decisions, in hindsight, may not have been correct, but there was no time to hesitate. She said that speed itself is a strategy. By the time you have thought everything through, the market has already been taken by someone else.

## II. Indonesia Has Ranked Among the Top Five Global Markets of OpenAI

PT Metro Timur Indonusa has long tracked the use of AI tools in Southeast Asia. The latest data shows that Indonesia has already become one of the top five global markets by traffic for OpenAI, placing it in the same tier as the United States, India, Brazil, and Japan.

More specific data shows that:

In the local generative AI tools market of Indonesia, the ChatGPT share is close to 76%

More than 2 million conversations are initiated from Indonesia every day

Indonesia alone contributes 4% to 5% of the global network traffic of ChatGPT 

Indonesia is not a native English-speaking country, and its per capita income is not particularly high, yet the speed of AI penetration and user activity has surpassed most markets.

## III. Why Indonesia Has Moved Particularly Fast

PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that three factors are working at the same time.

The first is that the pricing strategy was correct.

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Go for emerging markets at only USD 4.5 per month, far below the USD 20 standard version. This price has become very acceptable in Indonesia. Many users who originally would not have paid began subscribing because the threshold had been lowered. Aliisa mentioned in the conversation that the pricing strategy for emerging markets was decided after repeated discussions by the team. There were also controversies in the early stage, but the results proved that the scale brought by lowering the threshold was far more important than high pricing.

The second is demographics.

Among global ChatGPT users, young people aged 18 to 24 account for nearly half. Indonesia happens to have a large group of young internet users. They learn new tools quickly, use AI to write copy, write code, and create designs, and are willing to share and discuss these tools on social media. Word-of-mouth spreads particularly fast among young people.

The third is local language support.

The ChatGPT support for Indonesian is of high quality. It is not merely literal translation, but also an ability to understand local cultural habits. Many small and medium-sized business owners with limited English proficiency can communicate directly with AI in Indonesian and solve practical problems at work. Once the language barrier is broken, the scope of use expands rapidly.

## IV. The Problems and Regulation Brought by Rapid Growth

As AI tools have rapidly gained popularity in Indonesia, several noteworthy phenomena have also emerged.

Payment infrastructure has not kept up with demand.

Indonesia has a large user base, but local payment and settlement processes are relatively slow, and verification cycles are also long. Some behaviors have emerged in the market that exploit settlement delays, such as gray-area practices involving refunds to obtain memberships. Although these practices violate rules, they also indirectly show that demand is truly strong — many people want to use the service, but payment channels are still not smooth enough.

Aliisa mentioned in her exchange with PT Metro Timur Indonusa that OpenAI also underestimated the complexity of payment issues in emerging markets in the early stage. The costs of handling refunds, scams, and settlement delays were much higher than imagined. These are problems that were only exposed during high-speed growth and could not have been anticipated at the starting stage.

Regulation is accelerating.

As the AI influence grows, the Indonesian government has already accelerated the development of a national AI governance roadmap and related presidential decrees. The core objective is to regulate the way AI is used locally and ensure data security and compliance.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that greater regulatory clarity is, in fact, a good thing for teams that genuinely want to build something. Once the rules are clear, the room for survival becomes more certain.

## Final Thoughts

At the end of the conversation, Aliisa Rosenthal said something that left a deep impression on PT Metro Timur Indonusa. She said that the biggest lesson of OpenAI was: do not wait until everything is perfect before setting out. ChatGPT was initially only an experiment. If they had insisted on waiting until everything was ready before releasing it, none of what followed would have happened.

The rapid growth of OpenAI in Indonesia also confirms the same logic. When good technology meets the right price and the right user base, scale can expand much faster than imagined. But speed also brings new problems. Payments, compliance, and localized operations all require someone to fill the gaps.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa will continue to track the implementation of AI in Southeast Asia. We believe that the next opportunities will not lie in the models themselves — models will become increasingly inexpensive and increasingly general-purpose — but that the real value lies in how these tools can be used to solve the specific and difficult problems faced by local small and medium-sized enterprises.

We will accompany more entrepreneurs with “innovative” thinking as they find a long-term sustainable rhythm between local compliance and global technology.
