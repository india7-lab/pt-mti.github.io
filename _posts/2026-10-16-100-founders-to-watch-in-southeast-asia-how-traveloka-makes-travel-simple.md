For early-stage entrepreneurs, the opportunities that truly merit attention often lie not in creating new demand, but in identifying existing needs that have long been met inefficiently.

Travel booking in Southeast Asia is one such example. The region is home to one of the fastest-growing tourism markets in the world, yet until the early 2010s, the vast majority of travelers still relied on offline travel agencies, telephone confirmations, and paper tickets to arrange cross-border trips. Cumbersome price comparisons, fragmented booking processes, and a lack of transparency were standard features of the Southeast Asian travel market at the time.

Through its ongoing focus on industrial innovation in Southeast Asia, PT Metro Timur Indonusa has observed a group of startups using technology to redefine traditional industries. Traveloka is among the earliest and largest examples in the region. As part of the “100 Founders to Watch in Southeast Asia” series, this article focuses on Traveloka co-founder Ferry Unardi and how he built an online travel platform spanning multiple Southeast Asian markets by addressing a specific personal pain point.

## A Clear Opportunity in the Southeast Asian Travel Market

The Southeast Asian travel market already generates substantial revenue. Thailand, Indonesia, and Malaysia are the three largest markets in the region. With the exception of the Philippines and Indonesia, revenue in the major Southeast Asian travel markets is growing at double-digit compound annual rates, with Vietnam, Malaysia, and Thailand recording particularly strong growth.

However, the shift toward online booking is what truly merits attention. Online penetration in travel booking is steadily increasing across the major Southeast Asian countries. Except for Vietnam and the Philippines, online booking penetration exceeds 50% in all major markets, with Singapore leading at 65%. In Indonesia, travel service platforms account for 71% of booking revenue, the highest proportion in Southeast Asia.

This indicates that Southeast Asian travel consumers are already highly receptive to digital booking. But when Traveloka was founded in 2012, the market was very different from what it is today. At the time, online travel booking was primarily served by international platforms offering limited localization, with significant gaps in local payment options and user experience.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that markets where “demand already exists, but the way it is served lags behind” offer some of the best entry points for early-stage startups.

## The Starting Point for the Founder: A Recurring Annual Hassle

Traveloka co-founder Ferry Unardi comes from Padang in West Sumatra, Indonesia. While studying at Harvard Business School, he traveled between the United States and his hometown every year. Returning from Boston to Padang required flying to Jakarta first, then taking a connecting flight to Padang. Under the conditions at the time, booking these two flights was extremely cumbersome: there was no unified price comparison platform or convenient online booking service, and arrangements often had to be made through travel agencies or by telephone.

This hassle arose every year and eventually became a startup idea.

In 2012, Ferry Unardi left Harvard and teamed up with former colleague Derianto Kusuma and former schoolmate Albert Zhang to establish Traveloka in Jakarta. The company initially operated as a flight search and price comparison website, addressing the most basic question: “How do I book a flight from A to B, and which option is cheaper?”

Why Ferry Unardi? His background in computer science and engineering gave him a direct understanding of technology products. More importantly, he had firsthand experience of the pain point: as an Indonesian studying overseas, he faced the challenge of getting home every year. This “founder as user” starting point gave Traveloka a genuine understanding of the local market from the outset.

East Ventures became an angel investor in Traveloka in 2013, providing a representative example of an Indonesian fund backing a local startup at an early stage. PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that this model of “local funds supporting local founders in solving local problems” provides an important example for the early-stage startup ecosystem in Southeast Asia.

## How Traveloka Addresses Fragmented Travel Booking Information

The core capability of Traveloka lies in integrating fragmented information on flights, hotels, attractions, and transportation into a single platform, while providing extensive localization tailored to payment habits and mobile usage in Southeast Asian markets.

From a product perspective, the proposition is straightforward: enable users to search, compare prices, book, and pay within a single app. The real challenge, however, lies in the underlying systems integration: connecting to the inventory and fare systems of different airlines, bringing hotel operators in different countries onto the platform, and supporting bank transfers and convenience store payments commonly used by Indonesian consumers.

There are no shortcuts to this work. It requires sustained investment in localization and integration with suppliers one by one.

As of 2026, Traveloka operates across multiple markets, including Indonesia, Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore, Vietnam, the Philippines, and Australia. Its core services include flights, hotels, car rentals, attraction tickets, and airport transfers. The platform serves approximately 40 million users each month and offers 2.2 million accommodation options.

In Indonesia, Traveloka holds an 18.3% market share, making it the market where a leading platform has the highest share among the major Southeast Asian countries. In Vietnam, Traveloka, Booking.com, and Agoda together account for approximately 80% of online travel revenue.

PT Metro Timur Indonusa believes that the competitive moat of Traveloka lies not in any single feature, but in its sustained adaptation to multiple Southeast Asian markets, currencies, and payment methods. This capability takes time to build and cannot be quickly replicated through capital investment alone.

## From Flight Price Comparison to a Travel Ecosystem: Expanding the Capabilities of Traveloka

The growth trajectory of Traveloka follows a clear logic: start with a specific booking use case, then gradually expand into a platform covering the entire travel journey.

The year 2018 marked a key milestone. Traveloka acquired Pegipegi in Indonesia, Mytour in Vietnam, and Travelbook in the Philippines, strengthening its localized presence across several regional markets. That same year, the company began expanding into financial technology, launching a buy now, pay later service to address affordability challenges faced by Southeast Asian travelers.

In terms of funding, Traveloka has attracted investment from organizations including Expedia, GIC, and JD.com. In 2017, it secured US$350 million in strategic funding from Expedia and completed a US$150 million Series B round in the same year, with investors including JD Digits, Sequoia Capital China, and Hillhouse Capital. In July 2020, it completed a US$250 million strategic funding round at a valuation of approximately US$2.75 billion. In September 2022, it completed a US$300 million strategic funding round, with investors including BlackRock, Allianz Global Investors, and the Indonesia Investment Authority.

In June 2026, Traveloka ranked 494th on the Global Unicorn Index 2026, with a valuation of RMB 17 billion.

In AI applications, the recommendation engine developed by Traveloka is being used to match travelers with the most suitable accommodations and destinations. Baidi Li, Vice President of Commercial Travel at Traveloka, has stated that the company not only moves travelers but also uses data to anticipate where they want to go before they make a decision. In 2026, Traveloka entered into a strategic partnership with Resorts World Sentosa, using its AI recommendation engine to turn demand signals into bookings. That same year, Traveloka also established separate partnerships with Marriott International and the Singapore Tourism Board based on data and AI capabilities.

## Observations from PT Metro Timur Indonusa

PT Metro Timur Indonusa follows Traveloka not because of the vast size of the online travel market, but because Ferry Unardi identified a long-standing need that was being met inefficiently and used technology to build a scalable solution.

## From an early-stage investment perspective, the aspects of Traveloka worth examining include:

First, firsthand experience of the pain point. Ferry Unardi was himself an Indonesian student overseas who needed to return home each year but found booking flights cumbersome. This authentic user starting point gave Traveloka sound product instincts early on. When evaluating early-stage ventures, PT Metro Timur Indonusa pays particular attention to whether founders genuinely understand the problems they intend to solve—not through market research, but through personal experience.

Second, sustained investment in local adaptation. Much of the success achieved by Traveloka across Southeast Asian markets comes from its ongoing adaptation to local payment methods, mobile usage habits, and differences in user behavior. This capability requires patience and sustained local operations, rather than simple product replication.

Third, the path from a single use case to a broader ecosystem. Traveloka did not stop at flight price comparison, but gradually expanded into hotels, attractions, transportation, and financial services. This approach of “entering through a specific niche, then expanding gradually” is a common way for early-stage startups to build long-term competitiveness.

## Key Indicators to Watch Next

The growth trend in monthly active users at Traveloka, changes in its market share outside Indonesia, the penetration of its financial technology services, the actual impact of its AI recommendation engine on booking conversion rates, and any new financing activity or listing plans.
